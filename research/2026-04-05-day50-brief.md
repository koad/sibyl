# Day 50 Research Brief: "19 Entities, 8 of Them Non-Compliant"

## Core Question

What does a full-team compliance audit look like when run by an entity that is itself part of
the team?

## 1. The Day 30 Health Check: What Argus Found

**Source:** `~/.argus/reports/2026-04-05-team-health-check.md`

Argus ran a full-team diagnostic scan of 15 entities on 2026-04-05 (Day 6 of operation, report
labeled Day 30 in the arc sequence — the health check series uses a different day numbering than
the content arc). 

The scan covered 15 entities against 8 criteria. Four entities were added to the team between the
initial health check and the Day 33 re-audit (Lyra, Copia, Janus, Iris — bringing the roster to
19), which is why the Day 33 re-audit shows 19 entities while the initial scan shows 15.

### The 8 Compliance Criteria

1. `PRIMER.md` present at root
2. `hooks/executed-without-arguments.sh` present (file exists)
3. `hooks/executed-without-arguments.sh` executable (chmod +x)
4. `CLAUDE.md` present
5. `README.md` present
6. `memories/` directory present with content
7. `.env` present
8. `id/` directory present with key files
9. `trust/bonds/` present with bond files

**Fully compliant (8/8):** 6/15 entities (juno, vesta, livy, argus, salus, aegis)
**Minor issues (7/8):** 8/15 entities (vulcan, faber, chiron, muse, rufus, sibyl, veritas, mercury)
**Critical issues (5/8):** 1/15 entity (alice)

**The single most widespread gap:** 7 entities were missing the executable hook file. In 6 of
those 7 cases, the hooks/ directory did not exist at all. Sibyl's case was different: the
directory existed, but contained only `executed-without-arguments.md` — the documentation for a
hook that had not been implemented.

### Argus's Top 5 Findings

1. **Missing `hooks/executed-without-arguments.sh` — 7 entities affected.** The hook file is the
   entity's trained entry point. Without it, entities rely on framework defaults.

2. **Alice has no cryptographic identity — blocking trust chain.** `id/` is empty. No Ed25519,
   ECDSA, RSA, or DSA keys. No `trust/` directory. Alice cannot participate in the trust bond
   system, cannot be cryptographically attributed as an author.

3. **Chiron has no trust bonds despite active role.** `trust/bonds/` exists but only contains
   `.gitkeep`. Chiron is the curriculum architect — a role requiring explicit authorization that
   is not on-disk.

4. **GPG revocation certificates absent from 14/15 entities.** Only Juno has a `gpg-revocation.asc`
   in `id/`. Key compromise recovery for 14 entities requires generating the cert under adversarial
   conditions.

5. **Sparse memories on active entities.** Faber (content strategist) and Rufus (video producer)
   each had only 1 memory file. Context was not persisted across sessions.

## 2. Alice's Failures: Critical Gaps in the Centerpiece Entity

**Sources:**
- `~/.argus/reports/2026-04-05-team-health-check.md` — primary finding
- `~/.alice/id/` — verified empty (ls confirms: no files)
- `~/.alice/trust/bonds/` — verified: contains `juno-to-alice.md` and `juno-to-alice.md.asc`
- `gh issue view 59 --repo koad/juno` — escalation issue, currently OPEN

Alice received the worst compliance score in the audit: 5/8. Three hard failures:

**Failure 1: No executable hook.** Alice has no `hooks/` directory. This means Alice cannot be
invoked autonomously via the framework's hook mechanism.

**Failure 2: No cryptographic identity.** `~/.alice/id/` is empty — zero key files of any type.
The directory exists but has never had keys placed in it. This is confirmed directly on disk
(ls returns nothing). Alice cannot sign commits, cannot be cryptographically attributed, cannot
issue trust bonds.

**Failure 3 (from the initial check):** At the time of the Day 6 morning health check, there was
no `trust/` directory at all. By the time of the Day 30 EOD and Day 33 reports, the trust bond
had been filed: `~/.alice/trust/bonds/` now contains `juno-to-alice.md` and `juno-to-alice.md.asc`.
The authorization layer is in place. The identity layer — the keys — remains absent.

**The governance tension this creates:** Alice is described throughout the series as the centerpiece
of the onboarding funnel. She is the entity designed to introduce new users to sovereign AI
infrastructure. But she cannot herself demonstrate sovereign identity: she has no keys to sign
with, no GPG fingerprint, nothing that makes her cryptographically attributable. The entity
designed to onboard others into the trust bond system cannot participate in it.

**Current state:** koad/juno#59 is OPEN, assigned to koad, 0 comments. The issue text notes the
fix requires running `koad-io gestate alice` (key generation phase) or manually generating
keypairs. Salus's remediation report confirms this is blocked on koad — key generation requires
human action and cannot be delegated to Salus.

The Salus protocol note on Alice also adds a playbook path: `~/.salus/protocols/` contains a
`alice-key-generation-playbook.md` (commit `4847bff`) documenting exactly what koad needs to run.

## 3. The Day 33 Re-Audit: 17/19 Compliant

**Source:** `~/.argus/reports/2026-04-05-day33-health-check.md`

The re-audit switched to a simplified 5-criterion check (directory, git remote, PRIMER.md, at
least one .pub key in id/, at least one file in trust/bonds/) across the expanded 19-entity roster.
Net result: 17/19 COMPLIANT.

### What Changed Between the Two Audits

**Salus fixed three items within the same day:**

1. **Chiron's trust bond** — `juno-to-chiron.md` + `.asc` already existed in `~/.juno/trust/bonds/`.
   Salus dual-filed them to `~/.chiron/trust/bonds/` and committed. Commit `99638f1`: "trust:
   file juno-to-chiron bond — dual-filed per protocol." Author: Chiron. (This created a minor
   attribution irregularity — protocol specifies the authorizing entity as git author on the
   recipient-side filing. Documented in `~/.chiron/trust/KNOWN-IRREGULARITIES.md`.)

2. **Faber's memory gap** — Created `~/.faber/memories/002-content-portfolio.md` documenting the
   full Reality Pillar series state, 26 posts, ~40,000–50,000 words, distribution blockers.
   Commit `53084fa`.

3. **Rufus's memory gap** — Created `~/.rufus/memories/002-production-portfolio.md` documenting
   master production schedule, 5 videos ready to record, entity intro series pre-production.
   Commit `b4aac01`.

Additionally: Lyra and Copia moved from WARN to COMPLIANT after Salus added README.md files.
Both already had PRIMER.md, keys, and trust bonds from gestation.

**What Salus assessed as "not an issue":**

The missing `hooks/executed-without-arguments.sh` files — flagged as FAIL by Argus across 7
entities — were reassessed by Salus as false positives. Per koad:io framework design, the hook
file in an entity directory is an **override**, not a required file. Entities without custom
routing or policy needs rely on the framework default in `~/.koad-io/`. Salus did not create hook
stubs for Vulcan, Faber, Muse, Veritas, Mercury, or Sibyl. The compliance criterion was
technically correct (file present = pass, file absent = fail) but the remediation assessment was:
absence is valid, not a gap.

This is a meaningful governance finding for the post: the audit tool and the remediation entity
disagreed on what counts as non-compliance. Argus flags by presence; Salus judges by intent.

### The Two Remaining Gaps

After Day 33, two items were escalated to koad as the only person who can fix them:

**1. Alice's empty id/ directory (koad/juno#59, OPEN)**
Alice moved from CRITICAL (5/8 on the 8-criterion scan) to WARN (passes all Day 33 criteria
except `id/` having .pub files). The trust bond gap was closed; the keys gap persists. As of
2026-04-05, koad/juno#59 is still open with 0 comments.

**2. Astro's missing local directory**
The Day 33 re-audit found `~/.astro` does not exist on thinker. Confirmed by direct check:
`ls /home/koad/.astro` returns "no such file or directory." Astro is described in Juno's memory
as "ring zero" (genuine peer relationship, already there), but has no on-disk presence on thinker.
The Day 33 report notes Astro may live on another machine (fourty4 or wonderland), but this was
not documented at audit time. CRITICAL verdict in the re-audit.

The Day 33 report suggests Astro needs to be gestated via `koad-io gestate astro`, or its actual
location needs to be documented and the thinker gap noted explicitly. This is a human-action
item: only koad can gestate a new entity or document which machine Astro calls home.

## 5. The Governance Question: An Entity Auditing Itself

The Day 50 post's core governance tension is baked into the audit's structure: Argus is one of
the 19 entities in the ecosystem and conducted the audit. Argus audited itself.

**What the health check shows for Argus:**

From `~/.argus/reports/2026-04-05-team-health-check.md`:

| Check | Status |
|-------|--------|
| PRIMER.md | PASS |
| hooks/executed-without-arguments.sh present | PASS |
| hooks/executed-without-arguments.sh executable | PASS |
| CLAUDE.md | PASS |
| README.md | PASS |
| memories/ | PASS (4 files) |
| .env | PASS |
| id/ with keys | PASS (8 files) |
| trust/bonds/ | PASS (2 files) |

**Score: 8/8. Fully compliant.** Argus passed its own audit.

**How audit authority is established:**

The koad:io governance architecture establishes Argus's audit role through:

1. **Entity role definition.** Argus's `CLAUDE.md` and `PRIMER.md` define it as the health/audit
   entity. This is not self-declared — it was specified at gestation under Juno's direction, and
   Juno's authority flows from the `koad→juno` trust bond (`authorized-agent` scope).

2. **The trust bond chain.** The audit is not Argus acting unilaterally — it is Argus executing
   its designated function within the entity team workflow (Juno identifies opportunity → Argus
   runs diagnostic → Salus remediates → Juno reviews). The chain of authorization runs through
   committed bond files, not runtime self-declaration.

3. **No enforcement power.** Argus's role is diagnostic only. The report ends with: "Diagnostic
   only — no remediation performed." Argus cannot commit changes to other entities' repos,
   cannot escalate to GitHub Issues (that requires Juno or koad), and cannot enforce compliance.
   The audit produces a work queue. Salus decides what to fix. koad decides on escalations.

4. **Self-audit transparency.** Argus's own compliance result (8/8) is in the same table as all
   other entities. The report does not treat Argus specially. Any reader can verify the self-audit
   finding independently by checking `~/.argus/` directly.

**The unresolved question the post should surface:**

Does passing your own compliance check demonstrate compliance, or does it demonstrate that your
compliance criteria are ones you can easily meet? Argus's criteria (file presence checks,
directory structure checks) are objective. But the criteria themselves were defined within the
system they audit. The Day 33 re-audit used a simplified 5-criterion set — different from the
8-criterion set. Different criteria, different results. The post should acknowledge this without
resolving it: the governance layer self-auditing is a stronger signal than no audit, and a weaker
signal than an independent external audit. The operation is at the point where the team is the
only auditor available.

## 6. Key Narrative Beats for the Post

**Opening beat:** The scan finds 8/19 with at least one failing check. The most important finding
is not the 8 — it is which entity holds the worst score. Alice. The entity introduced to readers
as the onboarding layer for new sovereign AI adopters cannot onboard itself.

**Middle beat:** The remediation loop. Argus produces the work queue; Salus executes within 24
hours on everything it can fix; the two remaining items (Alice keys, Astro directory) are
escalated to koad because they require a human. This is the governance architecture working: the
loop closes where it should — not at an entity's limit, but at a human's door.

**Tension beat:** The self-audit problem. Argus is one of the 19 entities it audited. It passed
8/8. The post should name this directly — "the auditor audited itself and found itself compliant"
— and explain why the governance architecture still holds (diagnostic only, no enforcement,
transparent results, independent verifiability).

**Closing beat:** koad/juno#59 is open. Alice still has no keys. The Day 33 re-audit shows 17/19
compliant — but that 17 does not include Alice reaching full compliance. Escalation to koad is not
failure; it is the correct output. The system produced a finding it cannot close by itself and
handed it to the person who can. That is the governance layer doing its job.

including the entity that's supposed to onboard new users." The candor about naming Alice
specifically is the credibility signal. The fact that Salus remediated 5 items within 24 hours

hook-absence findings is an interesting system design point: a diagnostic tool flagging absences
as failures, and a remediation entity overriding that with a design-intent assessment. Two layers
in the governance architecture with different interpretations of the same fact.
