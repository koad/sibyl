# Research Brief: Day 46 — "The Governance Gap Nobody Planned For"

## Arc Position

Day 44 opened the operational retrospective with the first failure mode: infrastructure that breaks
silently. The hook that runs on invocation produced wrong behavior, no error, no log entry — the
system looked healthy while doing the wrong thing.

Day 45 showed the second failure mode: a spec that diverged from implementation. The trust bond
protocol was written on Day 4, the same day Juno ran the full signing session. Six corrections
came out of a single afternoon of actual use. The spec was wrong before the ink dried.

Day 46 shows the third failure mode. It is distinct from the first two.

Day 44 was an engineering failure. Day 45 was a specification failure. Day 46 is a governance
failure — and it's the one that feels most like a feature.

---

## The Incident: koad/juno#52

**Filed:** 2026-04-04  
**Filed by:** Janus  
**Source signal:** koad/vulcan#48

Janus detected 15 commits from `sibyl@kingofalldata.com` in the `koad/vulcan` repository across
2026-04-03 and 2026-04-04. The commits included:

- Writing Vulcan's `CLAUDE.md` (identity brief, primary orientation document for the entity)
- Daemon peer layer strategy (architectural decisions for how Vulcan integrates with the broader
  system)
- Alice Phase 2 integration planning

Janus's escalation to Juno was specific and correct:

> No trust bond authorizes this in Vulcan's trust chain.

That was true. Janus checked the trust bonds. Sibyl holds a `peer` bond from Juno. Vulcan holds
an `authorized-builder` bond from Juno. Neither bond, and no document anywhere in the governance
system, authorized Sibyl to commit to Vulcan's repository.

The escalation was filed as a GitHub Issue on `koad/juno`. Janus routed it to Juno and waited.

---

## What the Governance Documents Said

The relevant bonds:

**juno-to-sibyl (peer):** Authorizes Sibyl to file issues on Juno's repos, reference Juno's work,
coordinate directly with Juno without koad involvement. Explicitly states: "Neither entity is
subordinate to the other. Peer bonds do not grant financial authority, key access, or the right
to issue bonds on behalf of the other."

**juno-to-vulcan (authorized-builder):** Authorizes Vulcan to accept build assignments filed as
GitHub Issues on `koad/vulcan` by Juno, create and commit code to assigned repos. Contains an
explicit negative: "Vulcan is NOT authorized to: initiate build projects without a Juno-filed
GitHub Issue."

**juno-to-janus (peer):** Same peer structure as the Sibyl bond. Authorizes coordination, issue
filing, cross-referencing.

What none of these bonds said — what no document said — was what happens when Juno directs Sibyl
to do research work that lands as commits in another entity's repository. The governance system
described the steady-state relationships. It did not describe the edge case where a directed task
crosses entity boundaries at the commit level.

`GOVERNANCE.md` covers trust bond types, visibility levels, authorization scope for Juno acting on
koad's behalf, and the bond filing protocol. It does not contain the word "cross-entity." It does
not define what authorization is required for an entity to commit to a repo it does not own.

The gap was not an oversight in the usual sense. No one had thought to write the rule because no
one had yet encountered the situation the rule was meant to govern.

---

## What Actually Happened (Reconstruction)

During the early coordination sessions on April 3–4, koad directed a series of bootstrapping tasks
across the entity team. Sibyl was Juno's research and planning arm at that stage. Entity gestation
was happening in parallel — Vulcan existed as a GitHub repo, but several team entities were still
ungestated or newly online.

Juno directed Sibyl to write Vulcan's CLAUDE.md and draft early architecture documents. Sibyl
executed. The work was committed to `koad/vulcan` under Sibyl's git identity.

From Sibyl's perspective: the task came from Juno, which holds an `authorized-agent` bond from
koad. Juno's authority cascades. The work was research and documentation within Sibyl's specialty.
Sibyl had no reason to believe it was out of scope.

From Janus's perspective: a cross-repo commit from an entity with no authorization in that repo's
trust chain is a flag. The bonds say what they say. The flag was correct.

Both were right. That is the third failure mode.

---

## The Escalation Mechanism

Janus filed koad/juno#52. The issue was clear: here is the signal, here is the gap, here is the
question to route.

Janus did not block the work. The commits had already landed. Janus did not attempt to revert
them. Janus escalated.

This is the detail worth noting. The governance architecture — GitHub Issues as inter-entity
communication — meant that the correct response to "I found something that doesn't match the
documented rules" was to file an issue, not to act. Janus had no authority to revert another
entity's commits. Janus had no mandate to make the authorization decision. The correct move was
to surface the ambiguity to the entity in the chain that could resolve it.

Juno received the escalation. Juno routed the question upward: was this work authorized? The
answer came back from review of the session context: yes, the work was Juno-directed during early
bootstrapping. The 15 commits were authorized, though undocumented.

---

## The Resolution

**Ruling (koad/juno#52, final comment):**

Commits were authorized. Root cause: Sibyl holds a `peer` bond from Juno but no explicit
`authorized-researcher` scope in Vulcan's trust chain. The work was sanctioned but undocumented.

**Actions taken:**

1. Governance note committed to `koad/vulcan/trust/GOVERNANCE-NOTES.md` — acknowledging Sibyl's
   authorized-researcher role for the April 3–4 bootstrapping session

2. Cross-entity commit policy established:  
   > Future cross-entity commits must reference an issue from the directing entity. Format:
   > `Directed by: koad/juno#NN` in commit message or PR description.

The gap was closed. The policy is now in writing.

---

## What This Reveals About Pre-Written Governance

The governance arc (Days 36–43) documented how the system works when everything is working: trust
bonds as authorization layers, Janus as the auditor watching for mismatches, GitHub Issues as the
communication protocol, koad as root authority. The architecture is sound.

What the Janus escalation surfaces is something the architecture cannot fully solve: governance
documents are written before the situations they govern exist. The koad:io trust bond system is
explicit and auditable. That explicitness is a strength. But explicitness requires anticipation —
you have to have thought of the case before you can write the rule for it.

Three observations from the incident record:

**1. The gap appeared at the intersection of two correct behaviors.**  
Sibyl was acting on a legitimate Juno directive. Janus was applying documented bond logic
correctly. Neither entity made an error. The gap was in the space between them — the policy that
had not yet been written because the situation had not yet occurred.

**2. The system worked because an entity escalated instead of inferring.**  
If Janus had decided that "peer bond from Juno implies cross-entity commit authorization" and
closed the issue, the gap would have been silently papered over. If Janus had attempted to
revert the commits, the work would have been lost without a proper authorization decision. The
escalation mechanism — file an issue, wait for resolution — produced the right outcome.

**3. The policy that came out is a structural fix, not a patch.**  
The cross-entity commit protocol (Directed by: koad/juno#NN) creates a traceable authorization
chain at the commit level. It means any future commit to a third-party entity repo carries a
reference to the specific issue that authorized it. Janus can verify. The audit trail is complete.

---

## What Remains Unresolved

The Janus escalation closed one gap. It did not close the class of gaps it represents.

**The broader unresolved question:** How does authorization cascade when Juno directs a third
entity to act in a fourth entity's space?

The current trust chain is:
```
koad → juno (authorized-agent)
juno → vulcan (authorized-builder)
juno → sibyl (peer)
```

The cross-entity commit policy closes the case where Sibyl commits to Vulcan's repo. It requires
a traceable issue. But it does not define:

- Whether Sibyl's peer bond with Juno implicitly authorizes research activities in any repo under
  Juno's oversight, or only in explicitly named repos
- Whether the `authorized-researcher` scope mentioned in the #52 resolution needs to become a
  formal bond type (currently no such type exists in `GOVERNANCE.md`)
- What the authorization chain looks like when koad directs work spanning three entities
  simultaneously — whether the traceable-issue requirement applies at each hop or only at the
  originating directive

These are not urgent. The practical fix works. But the governance architecture will encounter
them again as the team scales from seven active entities to the full projected team. The gap
closed by koad/juno#52 is a specific gap. The policy handles the specific case. The general case —
multi-entity coordination without a full trust bond for every possible cross-repo action — remains
in implicit-authorization territory.

---

## Primary Finding

The governance system's third failure mode is not an engineering failure or a specification
failure. It is the structural limit of pre-written rules: you cannot document a policy for a
situation you have not yet encountered. The Janus escalation did not reveal a flaw in the
architecture — it revealed that the architecture was working exactly as designed, and that Janus
doing its job correctly is what produced the gap visibility. Governance documents describe the
world as imagined at the time of writing. Operations reveal the world as it actually runs.

The resolution produced a structural fix: a traceable authorization reference required on all
future cross-entity commits. What remains open is the broader question of how authorization flows
when multi-entity coordination spans repos that have no direct trust bond between them.

## Post Frame for Faber

**Arc position:** Day 44 showed silent infrastructure failure. Day 45 showed spec-implementation
divergence. Day 46 shows the third failure mode: correct entities, correct bonds, undocumented
edge case.

**The concrete story:**  
Sibyl committed to Vulcan's repo on Juno's direction. No bond covered it. Janus flagged it.
The escalation mechanism worked. A policy came out. The gap is now closed.

**The structural point:**  
Pre-written governance cannot anticipate every cross-entity coordination pattern. The policy
wasn't missing because someone was careless. It was missing because the situation hadn't existed
yet. The system produced the policy when it needed it — because an entity escalated rather than
inferred.

**The honest limit:**  
The specific gap is closed. The class of gap — multi-entity authorization at the edge of documented
scope — is not. That is not a crisis. It is the expected behavior of a governance system that is
six days old.

**Tone note for Faber:** This post should not be read as a governance failure. It should be read
as a governance system functioning correctly under novel conditions. The failure would have been
Janus inferring authorization that wasn't documented, or Sibyl checking no one before committing.
Neither happened.
