## Arc Analysis: Why the Verification Problem Is Day 42

Day 41's closing line is a claim: the entity IS the files, the keys, the trust bonds. Not the transport.

That claim has a natural counterpoint that any skeptical reader carries into Day 42: **"OK, but how do I know the entity is what you say it is? You wrote those files. You signed those bonds. Where does an outside party stand?"**

This is the verification problem. It has never been directly addressed in the series. And the answer involves:

1. **canon.koad.sh/juno.keys** — the public key distribution mechanism. An independent party can fetch Juno's Ed25519 public key from here without trusting koad's GitHub account.
2. **GPG fingerprints** — each entity's key fingerprint is the ground truth. Commits signed by Juno are verifiable against the fingerprint published at canon.koad.sh, not against GitHub's key storage.
3. **Keybase** — for human entities (koad), Keybase links GitHub identity + Twitter identity + the GPG key in a cross-platform proof. Social verification layer on top of cryptographic keys.
4. **The trust bond as a verifiable document** — anyone with GPG installed can run `gpg --verify juno-to-vulcan.md.asc` and confirm the bond was signed by koad's key. The bond is not just an internal authorization document — it's a claim that can be checked externally.
5. **The Alice certificate path** — Phase 2B means Alice's graduation certificates will be GPG-signed with Alice's own keys (`identity-init`). A graduate can verify their certificate against Alice's published fingerprint without trusting koad. This is the practical proof that the verification system is real.

This is **new architecture the series hasn't explained**, it flows directly from Day 41's closing claim, and it has concrete technical evidence (canon.koad.sh, gpg --verify, the Phase 2B Alice cert spec in vulcan#55).

## Recommended Angle for Day 42

**Title candidate:** "How to Verify an Entity That Claims to Be Sovereign"

**Alternate title:** "The Public Key Is the Identity"

**Core argument:**
Day 41 said an entity is its files, keys, and bonds — not its transport. Day 42 answers the obvious follow-up: what does that mean for verification? Not internal trust (that's what bonds are for), but external verification — what a third party, a future sponsor, or a skeptical developer can actually do with the public record.

The answer is: a lot. Here's how:

1. Fetch the public key from canon.koad.sh (not GitHub, not the entity's repo — from the operator's canonical domain).
2. Verify commits in any entity's git history against that key.
3. Verify trust bonds with `gpg --verify` — the authorization chain is in the file, checkable by anyone.
4. For Alice graduates (Phase 2B): verify the certificate against Alice's published fingerprint.

The arc payoff: the series has been building toward this. Day 3 said trust bonds aren't policy. Day 36 showed three bond files. Day 37 showed what happens before the first token. Day 41 said the entity is the files, not the transport. Day 42 shows that "files" means *cryptographically verifiable files* — which is an entirely different claim from "files you have to trust me about."

**What makes this strong:**
- It's the natural question a skeptical reader has been holding since Day 3
- It has concrete, runnable evidence (canon.koad.sh, gpg --verify commands)
- It connects to Alice Phase 2B (timely — vulcan#55 and #29 are active)
- Sibyl's `2026-04-05-sovereign-identity-standards.md` provides deep background
- It's the cleanest setup for the rings post that follows (the ring is the set of entities whose keys you trust)
