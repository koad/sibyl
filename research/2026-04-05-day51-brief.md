## The Aegis Design: What This Entity Is

Aegis is the security assessment entity for the koad:io team. Its mandate is narrow and explicit:

1. Find gaps between what the operation claims to be doing and what it is actually doing
2. Issue a public verdict with evidence and corrective questions
3. Return for follow-up sessions to assess whether prior findings were addressed

Aegis does not build. Aegis does not fix. Aegis assesses. The separation of roles is structural —
an entity that both identifies problems and resolves them loses independence. The value of Aegis
is precisely that it has no stake in making the operation look better than it is.

**What Aegis is authorized to read:** Any committed file in any entity directory. Session logs,
roadmaps, business models, trust bonds. No writes, no execution, no delegation.

**What Aegis is not authorized to do:** Override decisions, reassign work, hold verdicts private.
All assessments are committed to `~/.aegis/assessments/` and filed as GitHub issues on koad/juno.
The record is public. The verdicts are permanent.

## The Meta-Question: An Entity Assessing Its Own Context

The Day 51 post needs to name something the arc proposal flags but doesn't fully develop: Aegis
assessed the system that Aegis is part of.

### What This Means in Practice

When Aegis filed the first assessment, Aegis was reading documents that described the trust bond
system Aegis itself operates within. Aegis's own keys — generated by koad-io gestate — were part
of the cryptographic identity layer being assessed. The hook that fires Aegis was part of the
hook architecture Aegis was reviewing.

The security audit entity is not outside the system. It is inside it, using the same infrastructure,
subject to the same failure modes. If the hooks had been broken (they were — FORCE_LOCAL=1 was
missing until Day 6), Aegis's invocations would have been routing to fourty4 instead of running
locally, and Aegis may not have known. If the trust bonds had not been signed correctly, Aegis
would have been assessing a trust layer that included Aegis's own unsigned bond.

The first assessment actually flagged this indirectly: Aegis noted that Juno's 2026-04-01
reflection had identified the Aegis gap as "the gap I feel most." The entity designed to surface
drift was itself not yet operational — and the operation's self-assessment was aware of it before
Aegis was.

### What the Day 30 and Day 37 Assessments Show

The arc proposal focused on Days 3–5 (the original three audits), but the git log shows assessments
continued:

- `2026-04-05-day6-assessment.md` — RECOVERING → STABILIZING (end of day upgrade)
- `2026-04-05-day30-assessment.md` — Day 30 milestone review
- `2026-04-05-day37-assessment.md` — Day 37 sovereignty review, verdict STABILIZING, path to STABLE defined

The pattern holds: Aegis kept assessing, and the verdicts kept moving. The Day 6 assessment
(same day as the Day 5 assessment, filed after a second session) was the sharpest single-day
escalation: Aegis upgraded from RECOVERING to STABILIZING in the addendum because the second
session delivered volume and quality inconsistent with an operation still finding its footing.

For Day 51, the story ends at Day 5 (RECOVERING) — the arc proposal scopes it there for narrative
clarity. But the fact that the cadence continued and the verdicts improved is the proof that the
system worked as designed.

### The Harder Observation

The Day 5 assessment contained a finding that the post should name directly:

> "The challenge now is not self-awareness or avoidance. It is dependency."

This is the design working correctly. Aegis's job is not just to find failure — it is to
distinguish between types of failure. DRIFTING means "the entity could act but is not acting."
RECOVERING means "the entity acted, the remaining blockers are external constraints." The
distinction matters for response: drift is fixed by behavior change; external blockers are fixed
by escalation.

Aegis named the distinction in the assessment. Juno's #53 responses then executed on it: the
consolidated escalation went out via Keybase, the Plan B distribution path was named (GitHub
Discussions), the Day 9 targets were re-framed as aspirational. The audit created the correction.

## What "RECOVERING" Means as a Verdict Category

The assessment framework Aegis uses has distinct verdict categories. Based on the progression
across the three audits, the series post should define what RECOVERING means — not as a soft
upgrade from DRIFTING, but as a structurally different state:

**DRIFTING:** The operation understands what it needs to do and is not doing it. The path is
available. The constraint is internal — avoidance, comfortable infrastructure work, roadmap-as-cover.

**RECOVERING:** The operation has acted on prior audit findings and corrected internal drift. The
remaining obstacles are external constraints (koad bottleneck, credential dependencies, third-party
blockers). The path is partially blocked by factors the operation cannot control unilaterally.

**STABILIZING** (reached by Day 6 addendum): The internal machine is demonstrably working. The
content pipeline is cleared. The trust layer is complete. The operation is no longer recovering
from early gaps — it is running at capacity against a single external constraint.

The Day 5 verdict is RECOVERING and not STABILIZING because the Plan B path (GitHub Discussions,
no credentials required) existed and was not executed. The gap between RECOVERING and STABILIZING
is the last mile: not credential blockers, not koad bottlenecks, but completing the delivery loop
from "draft on disk" to "content that a human outside the operation can find."
